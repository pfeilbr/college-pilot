💔 Divorced parents: the rule that changes everything

Since the 2024 FAFSA overhaul, the FAFSA counts one parent: the one who provides the most financial support (usually the parent the student lives with). The other parent's income is invisible to it.

  • FAFSA ONLY Delaware, Penn State, Pitt, Rutgers, Ohio State, Michigan State, Indiana, South Carolina & UMass (all nine publics) — aid is computed on the one FAFSA household. A moderate custodial income reads as high need here no matter what the other parent earns. The out-of-state publics lean on merit (stat-based, income-blind) rather than need, so a strong transcript matters as much as the income line.
  • CSS + BOTH PARENTS Fordham, Northeastern, BU, Tufts — these use the CSS Profile with noncustodial-parent information. Both households' income and assets count toward institutional aid. If the other parent earns well, the generous "meets 100% of need" pledges shrink fast.
  • Waivers exist but are strict: CSS noncustodial waivers are for no-contact/abandonment situations — not for "he won't pay." Divorce decrees saying who pays for college don't bind the aid formulas.

🐘 Pennsylvania money that travels (or doesn't)

  • PA State Grant (PHEAA): income-based, up to ~$5,750/yr — works at the PA schools (Penn State, Pitt) and is portable to Delaware, Massachusetts, and Ohio (UD, UMass, Northeastern, BU, Tufts, and Ohio State) but NOT to New Jersey, Michigan, Indiana, South Carolina, or New York — so Rutgers, Michigan State, Indiana, South Carolina, and Fordham can't use it. A moderate-income household often qualifies for a partial grant.
  • PA 529 plans spend anywhere, with a PA state-tax deduction on contributions.
  • American Opportunity Tax Credit: up to $2,500/yr back at tax time for tuition payers under the income caps.
  • Federal baseline everyone gets: $5,500 first-year federal loan (rising yearly), plus work-study eligibility where need exists.

🎯 The strategy in one paragraph

For a family where the FAFSA parent earns a moderate income: if the combined-parent picture is also moderate, the CSS need-met schools (BU, Northeastern, Tufts) can drop below the publics. If the noncustodial household earns a lot, those schools jump toward sticker and the winners become the FAFSA-only publics — with Pitt as the affordability anchor (~$38K in-state sticker, ~$23K net at moderate income, direct-admit business), Delaware/UMass adding automatic merit, and the out-of-state Big Ten/SEC publics competing on stat-based merit: Indiana (Kelley) and South Carolina (Darla Moore) are the most generous and can land in the mid-$40Ks; Ohio State adds real merit plus the portable PA grant; Rutgers is closest to home but stingiest on out-of-state aid. Penn State's own aid is famously thin — its sticker barely bends. Run the NPCs both ways before ranking anything.

🗓️ Aid deadlines to calendar

  • Junior year (2026–27): run every NPC twice — once with custodial income only, once combined — before building the final list
  • Oct 1, 2027: CSS Profile opens (needed for the four privates; ~$25/school, fee waivers exist)
  • Fall 2027: FAFSA opens — file early; PA State Grant deadline typically May 1 for fall
  • With each ED/EA deadline: aid forms are due at or near the application date — late forms mean late (worse) packages
  • Spring 2028: compare official offers line-by-line; appeal with competing offers where reasonable

How to use this app

📱 Install it

Add to your home screen (☰ menu → "Add to home screen") — it installs as an app, works offline once loaded, and each school guide has one-tap directions and admissions contacts for the visit day.

🔄 Keep it fresh

The ☰ menu's Update app button pulls the latest version instantly. The version stamp at the bottom of the menu tells you what you're running.

⚖️ Trust but verify

Every number is sourced (see each guide's Sources section) and Reddit quotes are real archived posts — but data changes annually and anecdotes are anecdotes. Confirm anything decision-critical with the school.
